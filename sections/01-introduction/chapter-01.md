# Preface — The Beginning

Preface — The Beginning

I began writing this book from a dialysis chair — three sessions a week, three hours at a time, with an uncertain future hanging over me. I was exhausted, often discouraged, and trying to build Percentile.One without feeling ready for the scale of what I wanted to create.

I live with chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and a list of other conditions long enough to fill a chapter of their own. As I write these words, I am waiting for a life‑changing operation. And in the middle of all this, I have been doubted, abandoned, and ridiculed by people I once trusted.

But I also have something far more powerful: a fire that refuses to go out. A desire to build something that outlives me. A need to leave a mark on this world, even if I have to build it from a place of pain.

The only thing I have ever truly known is how to write software. I’ve been doing it for more than twenty years. But when the idea for Percentile.One arrived, I wasn’t ready — not technically, not architecturally, not in the way a platform of this scale demands.

To build what I envisioned, I had to master:

cloud development

reactive, message‑driven architecture

React and agentic UI patterns

Azure SQL, Cosmos DB, and data lake design

mobile development with .NET MAUI

and more that I would discover along the way

I wasn’t an expert in any of these. I was a traditional developer — C#, SQL Server, HTML, CSS — the comfortable stack I had lived in for decades.

But I was alone in my bedroom, and I had a choice: wait until I felt ready, or begin with what I had.

I chose to begin.

I learned on the job. I made mistakes. I rebuilt things. I asked the right questions. And I leaned on the one constant companion that never doubted me: Copilot — not just as a coding assistant, but as a thinking partner when I needed clarity most.

This book is not just about building a world‑class digital platform. It is about building one while life is falling apart around you. It is about refusing to quit. It is about turning pain into architecture, and adversity into design.

What follows is not a polished success story. It is the record of how a platform was conceived, designed, and built under real conditions — with limited resources, imperfect knowledge, and a determination to rise anyway.

This is the story of Percentile.One — and the story of the person who built it.
